Estate Inventory Template — Free and Automatic

A probate inventory lists everything the estate owns, valued as of the date of death. Most courts also want each asset classified and supported by a valuation source. A spreadsheet can hold that data, but it never totals itself or flags what you missed. The free Estate Ledger does both — right in your browser.

The template problem with spreadsheets

A spreadsheet template gives you empty boxes. It never warns you that an asset has no value entered, never separates non-probate transfers from the probate total, and if you delete a row your formulas break. Executor forums are full of stories about reconciling a broken spreadsheet months later.

The Estate Ledger template is the tool itself: enter assets once, get a clean printable inventory, and the same records flow into your final accounting at the end of probate. Nothing is retyped.

What "date-of-death value" means

Courts value the estate as it stood on the date of death — not today's prices. Real property needs an appraisal (or current tax assessment, depending on your court); bank and brokerage accounts need a statement showing the balance on that date. Personal property usually takes a good-faith market estimate. The Ledger records which source you used for every asset, so the number is always supported.

Common mistakes the Ledger prevents

  • Counting TOD/beneficiary assets in the probate total — the Ledger keeps them listed separately
  • "Unknown" ownership guessed as "no" — unverified assets stay flagged until you confirm the paperwork
  • Assets valued at zero by default — missing values are flagged, not silently ignored
