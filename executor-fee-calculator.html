Three kinds of fee rules

  • Statutory tiers — declining percentage of the estate (CA, NY, FL, NV and others). The schedule is the default; courts can adjust.
  • Receipts-and-disbursements basis — percentage of money actually received and paid out during administration (GA, TX, NC, AL). The estate value alone doesn't determine those states' fee.
  • Reasonable compensation — most remaining states: courts approve what is customary locally, commonly 1.5%–5% of the probate estate.

The gross-value surprise (California)

California calculates on the gross estate: a $1M home with a $900K mortgage counts as a $1M asset. And the fee schedule applies twice — once for the executor (§10800) and once for the attorney (§10810).

The fee is taxable income

Executor compensation is taxable income on the executor's own return; the inheritance itself generally is not. Executors who are also beneficiaries often waive the fee for tax reasons.
